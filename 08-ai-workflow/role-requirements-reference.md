# Target Role Requirements — Reference Summary

**Purpose:** This case study was designed against a specific Business Analyst role (client-facing BA at a digital product design agency) to make sure the practice work maps to real job requirements rather than a generic RFP exercise. This file summarizes, in my own words, the responsibility areas I designed the project against — it is not a reproduction of any employer's job posting.

## Responsibility areas the project is designed to evidence

1. **Translating ambiguous inputs into scope** — briefs, RFPs, existing products, or rough ideas turned into a clear, estimable digital product scope.
2. **Research-heavy, client-facing work** — investigating unfamiliar businesses/industries, asking the right questions, structuring requirements.
3. **Cross-functional collaboration** — working across Sales, Design, Development, and PM to validate scope and ensure clean handoff.
4. **Gap and risk identification** — surfacing assumptions, dependencies, risks, and unanswered questions rather than letting them hide in the document.
5. **Market-specific scope factors** — localization, RTL, accessibility, and GCC-region requirements where relevant.
6. **Discovery from client/sales conversations** — identifying the real business need behind a requested feature or solution.
7. **Product definition** — features, user roles, workflows, journeys, screen flows, functional scope.
8. **Procurement and proposal artifacts** — questionnaires, proposals, RFP/RFQ responses, estimation sheets.
9. **Effort estimation and phasing** — UX/UI and development effort estimated and structured into delivery phases.
10. **AI-assisted workflow** — using AI tools for research, analysis, documentation, and estimation, with human validation of every output.
